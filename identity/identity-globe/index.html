Identity Globe: From Threat Signals to Business Priorities

Explore the Identity Exposure Observatory: a live-source experiment in identity intelligence, evidence quality, and risk-based prioritization.

A breach headline is a signal, not a risk assessment. A report about exposed credentials, stolen sessions, or extortion can be relevant to an enterprise long before its own incident response team has confirmed an impact. But volume is not severity, and a point on a map does not tell us whether our business is exposed.

I built the Identity Exposure Observatory as an experiment in bringing external identity-related reports closer to the decisions architects, security operations teams, and business owners actually need to make. Instead of collecting another feed of alarming headlines, I want to ask: Which signals should change what we investigate, protect, or fund next?

Why timely intelligence changes priorities

A new report can alter the urgency of an existing weakness. A credential leak involving a supplier, a campaign targeting an authentication method we still support, or a breach affecting a platform used by our workforce can all change the order of work. That is especially important when remediation capacity is limited.

The practical value emerges when intelligence is weighed against business context:

  1. Exposure: Do we use the affected service, supplier, identity provider, or authentication pattern? Are our accounts, endpoints, or customers plausibly in scope?
  2. Evidence: Is this a disclosed breach, an extortion claim, or a secondary catalogue entry? Has the reported credential type been confirmed, inferred, or left unknown?
  3. Impact: Could the exposure enable account takeover, privileged access, disruption, fraud, or regulatory consequences? Which critical business processes depend on the affected identities?
  4. Actionability: Can we revoke sessions, rotate exposed secrets, strengthen FIDO-based authentication, constrain privileges, investigate anomalous access, or contact a supplier now?
PUBLIC REPORTS     +     INTERNAL CONTEXT       =     DECISION SUPPORT
---------------          ----------------          ----------------
Breach catalogue         Asset / supplier map       Investigate first
Extortion claim          Identity criticality       Reduce exposure
Credential signal        Control coverage           Validate impact
Source confidence        Business dependency        Allocate effort

The point is not to turn public reporting into an automatic risk score. It is to make evidence, uncertainty, and the next security decision visible together. A high-priority marker is a modeled signal for investigation, not proof that a company or country has suffered a confirmed compromise.

Explore the Identity Exposure Observatory

The working proof of concept below uses a standalone HTML and Canvas application. Its source selector includes Have I Been Pwned, MyRecon, and Ransomwhere, with an aggregate view. MyRecon can overlap with HIBP, while Ransomwhere includes extortion claims that are not necessarily independently verified. Availability depends on whether each external endpoint permits browser requests; the dashboard reports source status rather than fabricating live records.

The Observatory is embedded at a width close to the article text for comfortable reading. The application can scroll within its frame when needed. For detailed analysis, use Open in new window or View full screen to give the globe and analyst panels more space.

Reading the signals responsibly

The globe’s geographic pins represent locations associated with reports, not the origin of an attack or the physical location of stolen data. The priority indicators are modeled rather than independently verified. The source cards can help explain a report, but an inferred credential category must not be presented as a confirmed exposure.

For enterprise use, the next step would be to join these external observations to a controlled inventory of applications, suppliers, authentication methods, privileged identities, and business services. That correlation—not the visual intensity of a hotspot—is what makes prioritization defensible.

This is also where the connection to The Password Is a Vulnerability. Cryptography Is the Patch. becomes practical: public reports may help us identify where reusable secrets remain an urgent liability, while FIDO2 passkeys, device-bound authenticators, PKI, and sound credential lifecycle controls help reduce particular paths to compromise. None eliminates the need for detection, incident response, or careful validation.

This application is an exploratory intelligence interface, not an authoritative incident database or an automated enterprise risk assessment.
