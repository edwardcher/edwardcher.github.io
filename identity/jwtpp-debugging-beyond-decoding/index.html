JWT++ Debugging. Beyond Decoding.

Why I built JWT++ Debugger: a practical JWT and OpenID Connect workbench that helps you inspect tokens, analyze claims, review configuration, and identify the next troubleshooting step.

When decoding isn’t enough

There is no shortage of JWT decoders. Most do a good job of splitting a token into its header, payload, and signature, making the claims readable within seconds.

The challenge usually comes afterward.

A decoded token might look perfectly reasonable, yet authentication or authorization still fails. Is the issuer correct? Does the audience match the application? Has the token expired? Is the signing key available through the expected JWKS endpoint? Or is the problem somewhere in the OAuth 2.0 or OpenID Connect flow?

Answering these questions often means switching between a decoder, discovery documents, API responses, browser developer tools, and protocol references. Each tool provides part of the picture, but connecting those pieces remains a manual exercise.

Why I built JWT++ Debugger

I’ve used several good JWT debugging tools over the years. Rather than replace them, I wanted to address the gaps I encountered when troubleshooting real identity integrations.

My objective was to bring token inspection, contextual analysis, and OpenID Connect troubleshooting into a single workspace, following a natural progression: Inspect. Analyze. Review. Act.

JWT++ starts with the token, but goes beyond displaying decoded claims. It brings related configuration and diagnostics into context, including an OIDC workflow view that helps connect protocol exchanges with the information being examined.

I also wanted to retain something from my Terminal First approach. The interface remains straightforward for occasional users, while Vim-style keyboard navigation provides a faster workflow for those who prefer it.

The result is a practical browser-based workbench designed to reduce unnecessary switching between tools and make the next troubleshooting step easier to identify.
