How is this generated?

Reference implementation: ordered label/value pairs, missing values represented as null, JSON serialized as UTF-8, then SHA-256. No IP, cookie or behaviour data included.

const bytes = new TextEncoder().encode(JSON.stringify(pairs));
const digest = await crypto.subtle.digest("SHA-256", bytes);
This approach can be improved with additional signals and normalization. Identical inputs yield identical hashes. This is not a measure of population uniqueness.

Why does this matter for identity security?
Passwords and other typed input. Unusual interaction patterns may prompt extra verification even when a password is correct. Never type a real password into this lab.
Bots and automated agents. Interaction signals may help flag some automated activity, but automation can imitate human input. Neither a match nor a mismatch proves who is present.
Passwordless and continuous assurance. Passkeys authenticate cryptographically. Behavioural signals may complement them during a session, including ordinary form entry and navigation, without requiring a password.
Private by design. This voluntary exercise collects timing and movement observations locally, only during training or comparison. No background monitoring, retained typed content or transmission of the profile. Results stay in memory until reload unless you export them.

Sample quality measures exercise completeness, not identity confidence. Consistency is an uncalibrated similarity score, not a probability that you are the same person.
From interaction to profile
Behavioural biometrics studies how people interact, rather than what they type. Keystroke timing and mouse trajectories are established research modalities. This lab implements a small, independently written statistical demonstration, not a reproduction of a published classifier.

What we actually collect
ObservationImplementation
Typing rhythmConsecutive keydown intervals in milliseconds; excludes repeats, composition and gaps of 2 seconds or more. No key values retained.
Pointer speedEuclidean distance between consecutive same-type pointer events divided by elapsed time, in CSS pixels/second; gaps must be below 500 ms.
Clicks and durationClick count and exercise duration support sample quality, not the similarity score.
Automation observationsCounts untrusted events and reports WebDriver when exposed. Neither establishes human or bot identity.
Training and comparison
Three accepted two-stage exercises form the demonstration baseline. A fixed sentence selected from 24 prompts is used for enrollment and comparison. The exported profile records its sentence ID; changing prompts requires a new baseline. Quality is the minimum of three capped ratios: 10 key intervals, 10 pointer speed observations, 4 clicks (no minimum time). The exercise also requires the exact practice phrase and four ordered targets. Keyboard and pointer observations are collected separately. A complete baseline is not proof of identity.
quality = round(100 × min(1, keys/10, moves/10, clicks/4))
center = median(training feature values)
MAD = median(abs(training values − center))
scale = max(1.4826 × MAD, 0.15 × abs(center), 1)
distance = abs(new feature − center) / scale
feature score = 100 / (1 + distance)
consistency = round(median(feature scores))
Only median key interval and median pointer speed enter the comparison. MAD is a robust spread estimate; the scale floor avoids division by zero and overly sensitive comparisons. The 15% floor and score mapping are engineering heuristics, not scientifically calibrated thresholds. The SHA-256 digest identifies the serialized profile snapshot, not a person.
How this relates to identity security
After a verified FIDO2 or PKI sign-in, a backend could associate an enrolled baseline with the authenticated account. New observations could contribute to a risk engine alongside device context and cryptographic authentication, prompting step-up verification when appropriate. Automation can imitate human input; a mismatch is not proof of a bot, and a match is not proof of identity. Never block solely on this demonstration score.
Research foundations & limitations
Killourhy and Maxion (2009) benchmarked 14 keystroke anomaly detectors across 51 participants and multiple sessions. Their work motivates repeatable tasks and independent evaluation. A 2025 survey reviews keyboard and mouse features and adaptation challenges. Neither validates this implementation.
Production evaluation needs separate enrollment and test sessions, genuine and impostor populations, device and accessibility variation, false acceptance/rejection rates, ROC curves and equal-error rates. Do not use this demonstration to authenticate, block users or identify AI agents. Imported profiles are untrusted and potentially sensitive.

Developer reference · APIs and limitations
Device: existing ordered browser observations. Behaviour: digest of the three-round baseline. Time: Date.toISOString(), Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone and Date.getTimezoneOffset(). Location: navigator.geolocation.getCurrentPosition(), only on request; coordinates rounded to one decimal degree before inclusion. The browser or OS may use its own location service. No external lookup is initiated by this section.
SHA-256(UTF-8(JSON.stringify(snapshot)))
Schema version and fixed field order define serialization. Missing optional signals are omitted. A changed timestamp produces a different digest; compare individual features statistically, not hashes. A digest is not anonymous, authenticated, or proof of uniqueness. Browser signals can be spoofed. No production risk score is calculated.
All snapshot processing is local. No automatic storage or transmission. Location is discarded when deselected or on reload.
